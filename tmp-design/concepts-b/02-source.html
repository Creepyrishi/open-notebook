Stolen Focus — Johann Hari (chapter notes)

Content ch. 4–5

Ch. 4 — The collapse of sustained readingp. 121

Hari traces the decline of deep reading to an environment engineered for interruption. The median screen session now lasts under a minute, and each interruption carries a re-immersion cost the reader never consciously registers.

Ch. 5 — Environment beats willpowerp. 143

The core argument: willpower is the wrong unit of analysiscited in 2 answers. Individuals blaming themselves for distraction are playing a rigged game — thousands of engineers optimize daily against their self-control. The realistic lever is environmental: remove the frictionless paths to distraction rather than resisting them in the moment.

Hari's parallel with public health is deliberate — we didn't solve contaminated water with personal discipline; we changed the water supply. Attention, he argues, needs the same shift from individual blame to systemic design.

Is Hari saying willpower is useless?
Source

Not useless — misallocated. His claim is that willpower is the wrong unit of analysis: you spend it winning individual battles while the environment guarantees you lose the war p. 143. The public-health analogy makes the position precise — discipline didn't fix contaminated water, redesigning the supply did.

So willpower still matters, but only downstream: first remove the frictionless paths to distraction, then the willpower you have is actually enough p. 145.
